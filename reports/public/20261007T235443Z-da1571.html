Conclusions

Written analysis by the repository owner for run 20261007T235443Z-da1571, not generated output. Every figure it quotes was checked against this report.

Rank 1 is Jev, at $0.000027 per message and 0.20 s at p50. Three variants share the top tier, and the rule breaks that tie by cost: OpenAI's Decisions API has the highest share fully right at 88.0%, Sonnet 5.5 has 86.0%, and Jev 84.0%, all within noise of each other. On intent alone, Decisions is ahead of Jev by +5.5 points (interval +2.0 to +9.5), a gap that is not noise, at $0.000067 per message batched and 0.21 s at p50; if intent matters most, Decisions is the stronger choice at about 2.5 times Jev's cost. Jev ranks first under both our definitions and Gemini's.

Every variant below gets the same facts: messages fully right (all four answers) and its gap against rank 1, the intent gap, its weakest question, cost, and p50 latency.

An interval that includes zero means the gap is within noise at this sample size; the fully-right gaps for both Haiku variants exclude zero, so those are real.

How far to trust this: it is one run over 200 synthetic messages. Claude models wrote the messages, proposed the label edits, and drafted all four definitions, so the Claude variants may be favored. Two checks from outside Anthropic found no sign of it. A blind relabeling by Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro agreed with 25 of 28 edited urgency labels and 13 of 14 edited contact labels, which shows the edits follow the definitions consistently, though not that the definitions are neutral. Under definitions Gemini wrote, Jev also ranks first and Sonnet 5.5 also shares the top tier, while Decisions does not. The relabeling was made before intent had a definition, which affects one of its 46 edited labels and its intent baseline; the outside-definitions check does not depend on our definitions. Prompts were not tuned for any vendor.

All four correct under the labels as generated and as frozen. This shows how much each result depends on the edits. The two definition sets

QuestionCurrent (Claude session)Outside (google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview)
intentWhat does the sender of this message want? Not a lead means anything that is not a real estate inquiry, such as spam, a vendor pitch, or a wrong number. When the message clearly asks for two different things and neither is clearly the main one, either one is correct.What is the primary purpose of the sender's message? Choose exactly one label from: buy, sell, rent, valuation, general question, not a lead. Apply 'buy' if the sender wants to purchase a property. Apply 'sell' if the sender wants to list or sell a property they own. Apply 'rent' if the sender is looking for a rental property or wants to lease out a property. Apply 'valuation' if the sender asks what their home is worth without explicitly stating they want to sell right now. Apply 'general question' if the sender asks about market conditions, neighborhoods, or processes without indicating a transaction. Apply 'not a lead' to spam, solicitations, or unrelated messages. For borderline cases: If mentioning both buying and selling, default to 'sell' as listing usually comes first. If asking for a valuation to sell immediately, choose 'sell'.
timelineWhen does the sender plan to buy, sell, rent, or move? Count when the move or transaction happens, not when an answer or a valuation is needed. Answer unknown if the message gives no time for it.When does the sender intend to complete their transaction? Choose exactly one label from: under 3 months, 3 to 12 months, over 12 months, unknown. Apply 'under 3 months' for ASAP, immediately, soon, or dates within 90 days. Apply '3 to 12 months' for timeframes between 90 days and one year. Apply 'over 12 months' for a year or more, or casual future browsing. Apply 'unknown' when no timeframe is hinted. For borderline cases: If a range crosses categories, pick the shorter timeframe. If they say 'soon', choose 'under 3 months'.
wants_contactDoes the sender ask for a reply addressed to them, by call, text, email, or a meeting? A post or reply on a website, portal, web form, or chat widget does not count.Does the sender request or expect a reply? Choose exactly one label from: yes, no. Apply 'yes' if they ask a question, request a call, provide contact info for follow-up, or ask to schedule a viewing. Apply 'no' if they say do not contact, just browsing, or if it is informational with no reply expected like spam. For borderline cases: Any question about a property implies a reply is expected, so choose 'yes'. Default to 'yes' unless they explicitly opt out or the message is not a lead.
urgencyHow urgent is the sender's request? Answer high only when the message names a deadline or forced move within five weeks. A general wish for speed with no date is normal.How urgent is the request? Choose exactly one label from: high, normal. Apply 'high' for explicit words like urgent, emergency, immediate attention, or hard deadlines within 30 days like an expiring lease or standing outside a property now. Apply 'normal' for all other inquiries, standard requests, or casual browsing. For borderline cases: 'soon' without a hard deadline is 'normal'. Only use 'high' if a short delay would clearly cause issues.
