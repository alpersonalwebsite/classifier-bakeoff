Dataset: 200 synthetic messages, written by anthropic/claude-opus-5.5 to match labels chosen first. The generator's vendor (anthropic) also makes variants in this run (haiku-batched, haiku-per-q, sonnet-batched), which the wording of the data may favor.

Ranking

Rank 1: decisions-batched and decisions-per-q (identical answers on every question).

Rule: complete variants are ranked by the share of messages with all four answers right. A variant whose 95% paired interval against the top remaining variant includes zero shares its tier. Within a tier, cheaper ranks first, then faster at p50. Variants with identical answers share a rank. The rule was set after the first published run and is not retuned.

Conclusions

Written analysis by the repository owner for run 20261007T194642Z-ba611a, not generated output. Every figure it quotes was checked against this report.

Rank 1 is OpenAI's Decisions API, which gave identical answers in its batched and one-question-per-call forms. Use it batched: that is the cheaper and faster of the two, at $0.000062 per message and 0.19 s at p50. Sonnet 5.5 ties it on accuracy and ranks second because the rule breaks ties by cost.

Every variant below gets the same facts: messages fully right (all four answers) and its gap against rank 1, the intent gap, its weakest question, cost, and p50 latency.

  1. Decisions: 84.5% fully right; intent 94.0%, also its weakest question; $0.000062 per message batched or $0.000081 one question per call; 0.19 s or 0.79 s at p50.
  2. Sonnet 5.5, batched: 84.5% fully right (+0.0, interval -5.5 to +5.5); intent +2.0 (interval -1.0 to +5.0); weakest question urgency at 95.0%; $0.001998 per message; 1.41 s at p50.
  3. Jev: 72.5% fully right (-12.0, interval -18.5 to -6.0); intent -14.0 (interval -19.5 to -9.0); weakest question intent at 80.0%; $0.000025 per message; 0.23 s at p50.
  4. Haiku 4.5, one question per call: 73.0% fully right (-11.5, interval -18.0 to -5.0); intent -1.5 (interval -5.5 to +2.5); weakest question urgency at 86.5%; at least $0.001463 per message; 2.80 s at p50.
  5. Haiku 4.5, batched: 64.5% fully right (-20.0, interval -27.5 to -12.5); intent +1.5 (interval -2.0 to +5.0); weakest question urgency at 82.0%; at least $0.000781 per message; 0.79 s at p50.

Jev and Haiku 4.5 one question per call share tier 2, so cost decides their order. An interval that includes zero means the gap is within noise at this sample size; the fully-right gaps for Jev and both Haiku variants exclude zero, so those are real.

How far to trust this: it is one run over 200 synthetic messages. Claude models wrote the messages, proposed the label edits, and critiqued the label definitions, so the Claude variants may be favored; the table of dependence on the label edits shows how much each result rests on those edits, but it does not test that bias. Prompts were not tuned for any vendor.

Dependence on the label edits

Before freezing, 46 labels were edited: the edits were proposed with the help of Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), after a second Claude Opus 5.5 session critiqued the label definitions, and each one was approved by the repository owner. No reviewer outside Anthropic's models has checked these labels yet. The change log is data/label-changes.md.

All four correct under the labels as generated and as frozen. This shows how much each result depends on the edits. It does not test whether the review was biased: every variant was given the revised definitions, so the change mostly measures how well each model follows that wording on the edited messages. Only an independent relabeling of those messages can test the review itself.
