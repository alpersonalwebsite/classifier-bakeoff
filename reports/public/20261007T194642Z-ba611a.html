Public edition · run 20261007T194642Z-ba611a · 2026-10-07 · dataset sha256:8c43a059d800a559 · questions sha256:2db74e1065302281 · cap $2.00 per variant

Dataset: 200 synthetic messages, written by anthropic/claude-opus-5.5 to match labels chosen first. The generator's vendor (anthropic) also makes variants in this run (haiku-batched, haiku-per-q, sonnet-batched), which the wording of the data may favor.

Ranking

Rank 1: decisions-batched and decisions-per-q (identical answers on every question).

Rule: complete variants are ranked by the share of messages with all four answers right. A variant whose 95% paired interval against the top remaining variant includes zero shares its tier. Within a tier, cheaper ranks first, then faster at p50. Variants with identical answers share a rank. The rule was set after the first published run and is not retuned.

Conclusions

Written analysis by the repository owner for run 20261007T194642Z-ba611a, not generated output. Every figure it quotes was checked against this report.

Sonnet 5.5 is the only variant in the top tier under both definition sets. Rank 1 goes to the cheapest variant in that tier: OpenAI's Decisions API under the current definitions, and Jev under definitions written by Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro. Most of that swing comes from intent, the one question the current definitions never define: Jev's intent accuracy rises from 80.0% to 98.0% when "not a lead" is defined to include solicitations, though its timeline accuracy falls from 97.0% to 90.0%. Before choosing a cheaper variant, check which definitions match how you triage leads; Sonnet 5.5 is the choice that does not depend on them, at $0.001998 per message under the current definitions.

Under the current definitions, rank 1 is Decisions, which gave identical answers batched and one question per call; batched is the cheaper and faster form, at $0.000062 per message and 0.19 s at p50.

Every variant below gets the same facts: messages fully right (all four answers) and its gap against rank 1, the intent gap, its weakest question, cost, and p50 latency.

  1. Decisions: 84.5% fully right; intent 94.0%, also its weakest question; $0.000062 per message batched or $0.000081 one question per call; 0.19 s or 0.79 s at p50.
  2. Sonnet 5.5, batched: 84.5% fully right (+0.0, interval -5.5 to +5.5); intent +2.0 (interval -1.0 to +5.0); weakest question urgency at 95.0%; $0.001998 per message; 1.41 s at p50.
  3. Jev: 72.5% fully right (-12.0, interval -18.5 to -6.0); intent -14.0 (interval -19.5 to -9.0); weakest question intent at 80.0%; $0.000025 per message; 0.23 s at p50.
  4. Haiku 4.5, one question per call: 73.0% fully right (-11.5, interval -18.0 to -5.0); intent -1.5 (interval -5.5 to +2.5); weakest question urgency at 86.5%; at least $0.001463 per message; 2.80 s at p50.
  5. Haiku 4.5, batched: 64.5% fully right (-20.0, interval -27.5 to -12.5); intent +1.5 (interval -2.0 to +5.0); weakest question urgency at 82.0%; at least $0.000781 per message; 0.79 s at p50.

Jev and Haiku 4.5 one question per call share tier 2, so cost decides their order. An interval that includes zero means the gap is within noise at this sample size; the fully-right gaps for Jev and both Haiku variants exclude zero, so those are real.

How far to trust this: it is one run over 200 synthetic messages. Claude models wrote the messages, proposed the label edits, and critiqued the label definitions, so the Claude variants may be favored; the table of dependence on the label edits shows how much each result rests on those edits, but it does not test that bias. A blind relabeling by Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro, a vendor with no variant here, agreed with 25 of 28 edited urgency labels and 13 of 14 edited contact labels, and with its labels on the edits every rank and tier stayed the same. Definitions written outside Anthropic gave no consistent shift toward or away from the Claude variants; they changed which cheaper variant shares the top tier with Sonnet 5.5. Prompts were not tuned for any vendor.

The two definition sets

QuestionCurrent (Claude session)Outside (google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview)
intentWhat does the sender of this message want?What is the primary purpose of the sender's message? Choose exactly one label from: buy, sell, rent, valuation, general question, not a lead. Apply 'buy' if the sender wants to purchase a property. Apply 'sell' if the sender wants to list or sell a property they own. Apply 'rent' if the sender is looking for a rental property or wants to lease out a property. Apply 'valuation' if the sender asks what their home is worth without explicitly stating they want to sell right now. Apply 'general question' if the sender asks about market conditions, neighborhoods, or processes without indicating a transaction. Apply 'not a lead' to spam, solicitations, or unrelated messages. For borderline cases: If mentioning both buying and selling, default to 'sell' as listing usually comes first. If asking for a valuation to sell immediately, choose 'sell'.
timelineWhen does the sender plan to buy, sell, rent, or move? Count when the move or transaction happens, not when an answer or a valuation is needed. Answer unknown if the message gives no time for it.When does the sender intend to complete their transaction? Choose exactly one label from: under 3 months, 3 to 12 months, over 12 months, unknown. Apply 'under 3 months' for ASAP, immediately, soon, or dates within 90 days. Apply '3 to 12 months' for timeframes between 90 days and one year. Apply 'over 12 months' for a year or more, or casual future browsing. Apply 'unknown' when no timeframe is hinted. For borderline cases: If a range crosses categories, pick the shorter timeframe. If they say 'soon', choose 'under 3 months'.
wants_contactDoes the sender ask for a reply addressed to them, by call, text, email, or a meeting? A post or reply on a website, portal, web form, or chat widget does not count.Does the sender request or expect a reply? Choose exactly one label from: yes, no. Apply 'yes' if they ask a question, request a call, provide contact info for follow-up, or ask to schedule a viewing. Apply 'no' if they say do not contact, just browsing, or if it is informational with no reply expected like spam. For borderline cases: Any question about a property implies a reply is expected, so choose 'yes'. Default to 'yes' unless they explicitly opt out or the message is not a lead.
urgencyHow urgent is the sender's request? Answer high only when the message names a deadline or forced move within five weeks. A general wish for speed with no date is normal.How urgent is the request? Choose exactly one label from: high, normal. Apply 'high' for explicit words like urgent, emergency, immediate attention, or hard deadlines within 30 days like an expiring lease or standing outside a property now. Apply 'normal' for all other inquiries, standard requests, or casual browsing. For borderline cases: 'soon' without a hard deadline is 'normal'. Only use 'high' if a short delay would clearly cause issues.
