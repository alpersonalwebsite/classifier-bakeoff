Dataset: 200 synthetic messages, written by anthropic/claude-opus-5.5 to match labels chosen first. The generator's vendor (anthropic) also makes variants in this run (haiku-batched, haiku-per-q, sonnet-batched), which the wording of the data may favor.

Conclusions

Written analysis by the repository owner for run 20261007T194642Z-ba611a, not generated output. Every figure it quotes was checked against this report.

For lead triage, decisions-batched is the variant to pick. It gets as many messages fully right as Sonnet (84.5% with all four answers correct) at $0.000062 per message against Sonnet's $0.001998, roughly 1/30 of the cost. It is also the fastest: 0.19 s at p50 and 0.28 s at p95, against Sonnet's 1.41 s and 3.45 s. On intent it trails Sonnet's 96.0% by 2 points, with a paired interval of -5.0 to +1.0, so that gap is within noise at this sample size.

  • Sonnet 5.5 is the accuracy ceiling, and the most expensive variant with a confirmed cost.
  • Haiku 4.5 is close on intent (95.5% batched) but weaker on the other questions, at 82.0% on urgency and 89.5% on wants_contact, so only 64.5% of its messages get all four right. It is enough for intent routing, not for full triage, and it costs at least $0.000781 per message (some calls returned no cost).
  • Jev is the cheapest at $0.000025 per message and nearly as fast as Decisions, and it is the best or tied for best on timeline, wants_contact, and urgency (97.0% each). It trails on intent at 80.0%, and that gap is real: -16.0 points against the best, with an interval of -22.0 to -10.5. The largest group of its misses, 17 of 40, are non-leads it reads as sellers, mostly vendor pitches (15 of the 17).
  • Batching costs Decisions nothing: the scores are identical, and one question per call is about 4x slower at 0.79 s p50. For Haiku, one question per call helps the questions other than intent (73.0% all four right), and costs at least $0.001463 per message against at least $0.000781 batched; both are lower bounds, so the true ratio is unknown.

How far to trust this: it is one run over 200 synthetic messages written by a Claude model, so the Claude variants may be favored, and at this sample size accuracy intervals are about five points either side, so only large gaps such as Jev's on intent are settled. Prompts were not tuned for any vendor, so a tuned setup could rank differently.
